Designing a pizzeria kitchen: oven, counter and routes

In a pizzeria everything revolves around the oven. First you decide which oven and where, then the counter, the fridge for the dough balls and the route the pizzas take to the dining room. Here you will find how to think it through, with or without a kitchen alongside.

The oven is chosen first

  • It is the appliance that drives everything: the oven's size, energy, output and position are decided at the start. The counter, the fridge and the way out for the pizzas are arranged around it.
  • The size is counted in pizzas per batch. Start from the pizzas to bake in the peak hour, not from the seats.
  • The distances from walls, furniture and other appliances come from the manual of the chosen oven.
  • It is heavy and bulky: check that it fits through the door and, with your technician, that the floor can bear it.

Electric, gas or wood-fired

  • Electric: you need electrical power. Look at the kW available on your meter or electricity bill. An electric oven needs its own extraction too: cooking vapours and fumes must be taken away.
  • Gas: you need a connection with the right capacity and a flue gas outlet, as the oven's manual indicates.
  • Wood-fired: it needs a flue of its own, separate from the hood's, and a place for the wood that is dry and out of the aisle.
  • How to choose: start from what the venue already has and from where the flue can run. If you don't know yet, the design can start all the same.

The pizza counter

  • Beside the oven, one step away, but not right against the mouth: you turn to load the oven, and the fridge does not take the heat.
  • The fridge for dough balls and ingredients sits under the counter or right next to it.
  • In front of the oven mouth, the space for the peel stays clear: the peel is long.
  • A single sequence: stretching, ingredients, oven, finished pizzas.

What not to put next to the oven

  • No burners, fryers or fridges right against the oven: it is a matter of heat. In between goes a neutral worktop, or the space the oven's manual asks for.
  • Dough mixer and flour away from the cooking and the wash-up area: flour makes dust and suffers from humidity.

The route of the pizzas

  • From the oven to the pass or the dining room without crossing the pizza maker's zone: whoever serves does not get in the way of whoever is loading the oven.
  • Dirty and clean on two routes, when possible: the dirty plates go back to the wash-up area without passing in front of the oven.
  • Open-view oven: customers like it, but the public does not enter the work zone: a separation stays between the counter and the dining room.
  • Takeaway and delivery: a worktop for the boxes and a way out for takeaway pizzas that crosses neither the dining room nor the pizza maker.

Pizzeria with a kitchen

If besides pizza you serve fried food, first courses or main courses, the oven and the pizza counter stay a block of their own.

  • The cooking line (burners or induction, fryer, griddle) sits under its own hood, separate from the oven.
  • The wood-fired oven has its own hood and its own flue: the fryer and the burners do not go under that hood.
  • Fried food: the fryer has a worktop next to it to put things down, away from the stretching counter.
  • There is only one pass: pizzas and kitchen dishes arrive at the same point.

Dough and refrigeration

  • A space for dough, even a small one, with the dough mixer and the worktop: on a worktop with a socket, not next to a sink.
  • Countertop machines according to the menu: besides the dough mixer, whatever is needed to prepare the ingredients.
  • Gluten-free pizza: if you want to make it, say so at the start: it changes spaces and equipment, and the rules depend on the place.
  • Refrigeration for the dough balls: refrigerated counters under the counter; an extra cabinet when volumes grow.
  • A cold room only if volumes are high.

Wash-up area

  • Separate from the pizza counter and the dough, near the way back from the dining room.
  • Hand-wash basin in the work zone, with non-manual controls.

The most common mistakes

  • Choosing the oven last, when its place and the flue have already been decided by something else.
  • Deciding the oven without knowing where the flue runs.
  • A fryer under the wood-fired oven's hood.
  • Pizza counter right against the oven mouth, or too far away.
  • A fridge or a fryer against the side of the oven.
  • No space for the peel.
  • Dough mixer next to the wash-up area.
  • Waiters passing behind the pizza maker to collect the pizzas.

What your technician checks

Flue, emissions, ventilation, floor areas and local regulations change from country to country and often from city to city. Your technician checks them: the equipment design does not certify the venue.
