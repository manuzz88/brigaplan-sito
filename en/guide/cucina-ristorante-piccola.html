The kitchen of a small restaurant: how to organise it in a few metres

In a small kitchen you cannot have everything. You choose what the menu really needs, you merge the workplaces and you use every worktop twice: on top to work, underneath to store. Here you will find how to think it through.

First the menu, then the appliances

  • Few cooking appliances, well chosen: every extra appliance makes the line and the hood longer. Start from the dishes you sell most.
  • Appliances that do several jobs: a combi oven replaces several cooking appliances.
  • The peak hour is what counts: appliances are chosen on the dishes to make at the busiest moment, not on the seats.

Cooking against the wall

  • A single line, against the wall: in small kitchens it is the normal choice. An island needs aisles on both sides.
  • Merged sections: first courses and main courses become a single workplace.
  • Worktops alongside: the oven and the fryer need a worktop nearby to put things down.

Refrigeration under the worktop

  • Refrigerated counters instead of cabinets: you store underneath and work on top.
  • No low fridges you cannot work on: a low unit without a worktop is wasted space.
  • A cold room only if needed: for small volumes, counters and one refrigerated cabinet are enough.

Oven and blast chiller one above the other

  • Oven on a stand with the blast chiller underneath: they take up the space of a single appliance.
  • Small blast chiller: if the larger one does not fit, the small one is fine, but with a worktop on top to work on.

The wash-up area

  • Separate from preparation, even if it is only a corner.
  • Near the way back from the dining room: the dirty plates do not cross the kitchen.
  • Dirty and clean on two routes, when possible: one door and a pass, or two doors.

The aisles

  • One comfortable main aisle that reaches every zone of the kitchen. In Brigaplan designs it is 80 cm.
  • Narrower secondary aisles: where only one person passes now and then, they can be narrower.
  • The doors: check that the largest appliances fit through the door, or that they can be taken apart.

Gas or electric in a small venue

  • Look at what is already there: the gas connection and the electrical power available, in kW.
  • Small venue and a lot of gas: the ventilation may not be enough. Moving part of the cooking to electric is one solution.
  • Electric: you need power. Check the meter before deciding.
  • Activated-carbon hood, with no flue to the outside: it can be considered only if all the cooking is electric, and where local rules allow it. With gas you need the flue; with wood or charcoal, a dedicated flue.

The mistakes that waste space

  • Too many appliances for a short menu.
  • Refrigerated cabinets where a refrigerated counter was enough.
  • No worktop next to the cooking.
  • Wash-up area far from the way back from the dining room.
  • An island in a kitchen that does not have the aisles for it.

What your technician checks

Minimum floor areas, heights, toilets and local regulations change from country to country and often from city to city. Your technician checks them: the equipment design does not certify the venue.
