Designing a bar: bar counter, back bar and equipment

A bar is designed around the movements of the person behind the counter: making a coffee, mixing a cocktail, washing the glasses, without taking one extra step. Here you will find how to arrange the bar counter, the back bar and the equipment, for a stand-alone bar and for the bar of a restaurant.

Where to start

  • What you serve: coffee, cocktails, wine, cold or hot dishes. Each one needs its own equipment. A display case, a griddle, an aperitivo counter and draught beer must be mentioned at the start: they need their own space and connections.
  • How many people work behind the counter at the busiest time.
  • The venue: dimensions, entrance, where the seating area is, where the water supply and the drains are.
  • Energy: the electrical power available in kW, which you find on your electricity bill or meter.

Bar counter and back bar

  • The bar counter faces the customer: you work at it looking at the room.
  • The back bar is behind you: bottles, fridges, sometimes the coffee machine.
  • The sink goes on the bar counter, not on the back bar: you work facing the customer.
  • The passage between bar counter and back bar must be comfortable for the people working; if two people work behind the counter, they must be able to pass each other.
  • Waste and empties: plan a place for waste and for empty bottles.

Coffee

  • The coffee machine has the coffee grinder next to it and the knock-out drawer underneath: it is a single movement.
  • The coffee unit also has a service drawer and a technical compartment for the coffee machine's water softener.
  • The coffee grinder away from heat and damp: not above the motor of a fridge, not next to the steam from the glasswasher.

Glasses and washing

  • The glasswasher goes under the counter, close to the point where the dirty glasses come back.
  • Treated water: for glasses without marks, a dedicated reverse osmosis unit close to the machine; for a good wash a water softener is enough. The water treatment sits in a compartment that opens, because it has to be refilled and replaced.
  • The hand-wash basin in the work zone, with non-manual controls.

Ice and refrigeration

  • The ice maker goes close to whoever makes the cocktails, not in the kitchen.
  • The drinks fridge goes in the back bar or under the counter, not where it opens in the customer's face.
  • A freezer for ice cream, desserts and amaro liqueurs, if you serve them.
  • Air for the motors: the ice maker and the fridges have a motor that gives off heat. The grilles stay clear, not closed inside the counter.
  • Water and drains: the ice maker and the glasswasher need a water supply and a drain; some fridges need a condensate drain. This must be decided before the counter is installed.

Raised floor or not

Bars used to be built on a wooden raised floor. Today there are two cleaner solutions.

  • On feet, at the same level as the rest of the venue.
  • On a masonry plinth, with the units resting directly on top.
  • The wooden raised floor can still be built, but we advise against it: dirt builds up underneath. With a raised floor the heights of the units change: this must be decided at the start.

The restaurant bar

  • In view: in most restaurants the bar is in the dining room, with glasswasher, coffee, ice and drinks fridge.
  • Hidden: in high-end restaurants it is often a closed service room, close to the dining room, with the same equipment.
  • Desserts: in many restaurants the bar handles them, with its own refrigeration for desserts and ice cream; in very busy restaurants they stay in the kitchen.

Bar with a kitchen

If you also make hot dishes, cooking follows the rules of every kitchen: the cooking appliances sit under the hood, and dishwashing is separate from the bar counter. See How to design a restaurant kitchen.

The most common mistakes

  • Sink on the back bar: you work with your back to the customer.
  • Ice maker far from whoever makes the cocktails.
  • Coffee grinder above a motor or next to steam.
  • Ice maker or fridge closed in without air for the motor.
  • Glasswasher far from where the glasses come back.
  • Water supply and drains decided after choosing the counter.
  • Raised floor decided last, with units of the wrong height.
  • Drinks fridge that opens towards the customer.

What your technician checks

Floor areas, heights, toilets and local regulations change from country to country and often from city to city. Your technician checks them: the equipment design does not certify the venue.
