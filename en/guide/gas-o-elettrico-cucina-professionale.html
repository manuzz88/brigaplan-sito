Gas or electric professional kitchen: how to choose

Gas or electric is not a matter of taste. It depends on what the venue already has, on what you cook and on how much it costs to bring in the energy that is missing. Here you will find the questions to ask yourself before choosing the equipment.

First look at what you have

  • Electrical power available: it is written in kW on your electricity bill or on the meter. It is the first number to look for.
  • Gas connection: is there one already? With what capacity? If it is missing, ask how much it costs and how long it takes to get it.
  • Flue and ventilation: where the flue can run and how air gets into the room.

When gas makes sense

  • The connection is already there and the electrical power is low.
  • Flame cooking: wok, open burners, some grills.
  • Increasing the electrical power costs too much or is not possible in the building.

When electric makes sense

  • You have enough electrical power, or you can increase it at a reasonable cost.
  • There is no gas or bringing it in is complicated.
  • The venue is small: with a lot of gas the ventilation may not be enough.
  • You want a cooler kitchen: induction heats the pan, and the room far less.

Gas and electric together: when you need it

A kitchen can use both kinds of energy: for example gas burners and an electric oven, or induction and a single gas appliance. Using both lets you stay within the electrical power you have.

What changes in the design

  • Services: gas needs its own line, ventilation and safety devices; electric needs a suitable distribution board, lines and power.
  • Hood: you need it in both cases. Every cooking appliance sits under the hood.
  • Activated-carbon hood, with no flue to the outside: it can be considered only if all the cooking is electric, and where local rules allow it. With gas you need the flue; with wood or charcoal, a dedicated flue.
  • Wall between kitchen and dining room: with a lot of gas power there may be fire-resistance requirements, for the pass too.
  • Appliances: the same appliance usually exists in both versions, with similar dimensions. But changing energy once the design is finished changes the connections.
  • Mains gas is not the same in every country: the appliance must be ordered in the version for your country's gas. Ask your supplier.

If you don't know yet

That's fine. A design can start with "I don't know": the choice is made from the venue's data, and it can be changed before the construction drawings. What matters is deciding before you order the appliances.

What your technician checks

Gas rules, ventilation, flue and fire safety change from country to country. Your technician checks them: the equipment design does not certify the venue.
