Commercial kitchen hood: size, airflow and ducting

The hood removes fumes, steam, grease and heat from the kitchen. It only works well together with two other things: the flue that carries the air outside and the fresh air that comes in to replace it. Here you will find what the standards say, what to decide at the start and what to ask the technician.

Where the hood goes

  • Over all the cooking: burners, griddles, fryers, pasta cookers and ovens sit under a hood (EN 16282-2, excerpt).
  • Wall or island: a wall hood sits over a line against the wall; an island hood sits over island cooking, open on all sides.
  • As long as the line: every extra appliance makes the cooking line longer, and so the hood too. That is why the appliances are chosen before the hood.
  • Wood and charcoal apart: a wood-fired oven, a charcoal oven and a charcoal grill have a flue of their own and do not connect to the hood. The details are in the guide Pizzeria with a wood-fired oven.

Hood dimensions

These values come from the European standard on kitchen hoods (EN 16282-2, excerpt) and from a German guide for kitchens (BGN ASI 2.19, §4.3, in German).

  • Height: the lower edge of the hood is between 2.0 and 2.5 m from the floor.
  • Overhang: with the edge at 2.1 m, the hood extends at least 30 cm beyond the appliances, at the sides and at the front. If the hood is higher, the overhang must be larger.
  • Ovens with a door: on the door side of combi ovens the overhang is at least 60 cm, because when the door opens the steam comes out all at once.
  • Grease filters: at least 45 cm above the cooking surface. Any lower, and a fire suppression system is required.
  • The ceiling: before choosing the hood, measure the height of the room and check the beams and services already above the line.

How much air it must extract

  • It depends on what you cook: the airflow changes with the type of appliances, their power, gas or electric, and with the size of the kitchen. The European standard gives the methods: an initial estimate and then the calculation appliance by appliance (EN 16282-1:2026, excerpt).
  • The real number is calculated by the technician: the design airflow is calculated by the building services technician or by whoever supplies the hood, on the chosen line.
  • In Italy, for example, with the gas kitchen in the dining room: if the gas appliances exceed 35 kW and stand in the same room where people eat, they must be connected to a hood with an extractor. The kitchen area must always be kept at negative pressure towards the dining room, with an extraction of at least 1 m³/h for each kW of the connected appliances (DM 8/11/2019, point 7.3.4, in Italian).

The air that comes back in: make-up air

The air the hood carries outside must come back in somewhere. If it does not, the hood extracts less, the fumes stay in the kitchen and the gas burners burn worse.

  • Slight negative pressure: the kitchen is kept in balance or at slightly negative pressure compared with the dining room, so odours do not leave the kitchen. The air coming in is about 95-97% of the air going out (EN 16282-1:2017, as reported by TZB-info, in Czech).
  • Clean air: the fresh air is taken from outside, away from waste and exhausts. It must not blow onto the workstations or disturb the hood (HSE CAIS10).
  • Small and large kitchens: the British HSE guide says that in small kitchens grilles in walls, doors or windows may be enough; in large installations you need a supply with a fan and a filter (HSE CAIS10).
  • No pedestal fans: according to the same guide, they are not a good way to bring in fresh air, because they disturb the hood.
  • In winter: the incoming air is cold. How to heat it, and how much it costs, is decided with the technician.

If you cook with gas

  • Gas linked to extraction: in Italy, for example, when the air is moved by a fan, the gas must shut off by itself if the airflow drops below the minimum, and it can only be turned back on by hand (UNI 8723:2017, point 6.1).
  • Openings for air: the permanent openings for gas and the rules on the room are checked by the gas technician.
  • Gas or electric: what changes for the hood and the services is in the guide Gas or electric professional kitchen.
  • In your country: the rules are different: check them with a local technician.

The flue and the outlet

  • The flue matters as much as the hood: a narrow flue limits the air the hood can carry outside, and so the appliances you can put under it. Ask right away whether there is a flue, how big it is and where it ends.
  • Faster means noisier: if the air in the ducts moves too fast, the noise grows. The European standard on ducts (EN 16282-5) gives indicative velocities, and the technician sizes the flue and the fan.
  • Materials: the ducts are made of materials that do not deform in a fire, such as steel or stainless steel, and they are not flexible hoses (summary of EN 16282-5, INSTAL 4/2021, in Polish).
  • Outlet on the roof: in many municipalities hoods discharge above the roof. In Milan, for example, they discharge into exclusive-use flues on the roof (Milan Building Regulation, in Italian).
  • Activated carbon: a recirculating activated-carbon hood, with no flue to the outside, can be considered only with all-electric cooking and where the municipality allows it. In Milan, proven maintenance is also required. With gas, wood or charcoal you need the flue.

Noise and neighbours

  • Noise like other installations: in Italy, for example, a restaurant's hood, motor and flue are fixed noise sources and must respect the limits of the noise law (L. 447/1995, art. 2, in Italian).
  • The limits depend on the area: under the same law, the municipality sets the acoustic class of each area with its own plan (L. 447/1995, art. 6, in Italian).
  • Near homes: if the flue runs next to homes or the motor is near windows, involve a qualified acoustics technician early. Typical solutions are silencers and a larger fan that turns more slowly.
  • In your country: the rules are different: check them with a local technician.

Cleaning and safety

  • Filters: grease filters are removed and washed often, as the hood's manual says.
  • Ducts: grease that builds up in the ducts can catch fire. That is why the ducts must be accessible for inspection and cleaning.
  • Access: leave room to reach the filters and the inspection hatches without dismantling the kitchen.

Mistakes to avoid

  • A hood shorter than the cooking line, or with no overhang at the sides.
  • A fryer or burners under the wood-fired oven's hood.
  • No make-up air: the hood is there but does not extract.
  • A flue thought of after the equipment: once work has started you find out it does not fit through or is too small.
  • Activated carbon with gas cooking.
  • Motor and outlet under the neighbours' windows, without an opinion on the noise.

How to get a free preview

Brigaplan generates a preview of the kitchen with AI and the engine checks it against the rules. In the full design and the construction drawings, the hood covers the whole cooking line, with an estimate of the airflow and of the make-up air for the technician. Prepare this information:

  • Floor plan or description: upload the floor plan of your venue, or describe it in words.
  • What you cook: the type of venue and the main dishes.
  • Energy and connections: gas, electric or both for cooking; the electrical power available in kW, which you find on your electricity bill or meter; whether there is already a gas connection. If you don't know these yet, that's fine.
  • The flue: whether there is one already, its diameter or cross-section, whether it reaches the roof. If this is missing, the engine gives a warning, not an error.
  • The city: the rules on the outlet and on activated carbon change from place to place.
  • What you get: a preview without dimensions, brands or product codes, with the engine's warnings.

The design is done online. Local rules must be checked with a local technician.
