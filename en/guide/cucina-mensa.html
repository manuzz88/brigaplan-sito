Canteen kitchen: food systems, zones and rules

A canteen kitchen is designed starting from one question: is the meal eaten where it is cooked, or does it travel? The equipment, the zones and the rooms depend on the answer. Here you will find the food systems, the temperatures required by some Italian rules as examples, the kitchen zones, the satellite kitchen that serves the meals, the dining room and the Italian rules for public tenders.

First choose the food system

The food system is the way the meal gets from cooking to the plate. The Italian national guidelines describe these types (Italian Ministry of Health, Guidelines for hospital, care and school catering, 2021, Annex 4, p. 39, in Italian):

  • Cook & serve: the food is cooked and served straight away.
  • Cook & hold: the food is cooked and the meal, hot or cold depending on the recipe, stays at the temperature conditions of production; it reaches the place where it is eaten in a short time and is served without any further heat treatment.
  • Cook-chill: after cooking, the food is blast chilled, it is kept cold and before the meal it is brought back to temperature.
  • Cook-freeze: like cook-chill, but the food is frozen or deep-frozen.
  • Sous-vide: the food is cooked at low temperature in vacuum bags, then blast chilled and regenerated.
  • Mixed: two or more food systems together.

The same guidelines prefer cook & serve and cook & hold (p. 13). For schools, the 2010 guidelines ask for meals to be produced in the school wherever possible and for the time between preparation and serving to be kept to a minimum (National guidelines for school catering, 2010, p. 20, in Italian).

The temperatures each food system requires

The food system decides which machines you need. Here is what some Italian rules ask for. These are examples from Italy. In your country the rules are different: check them with a local technician and with the local authorities.

  • Cook & hold: in Lombardy (Italy), the meal production centre must be able to keep cooked food at +65 °C (Lombardy model hygiene regulation, art. 4.5.11, in Italian). For schools, ATS Insubria (the local health authority) indicates hot dishes above +65 °C and cold dishes below +10 °C, for at most 2-3 hours before the meal; with cook & serve, the meal is eaten within 15 minutes of the end of cooking (ATS Insubria, Guidelines for school catering, 2023, p. 5, in Italian).
  • Cook-chill: in Italy, the national guidelines speak of chilling below +10 °C at the core within about 90 minutes (Annex 4, p. 39). The Lombardy regulation asks for +8 °C at the core to be reached in 3 hours and for storage at +2 °C in appliances fitted with a temperature recorder (art. 4.5.11). The two sources give different times: ask the local health authorities which value they apply.
  • Cook-freeze: in Lombardy (Italy), -18 °C at the core in 4 hours and storage at -20 °C, with a temperature recorder (art. 4.5.11).
  • What it means for the kitchen: with cook & hold you need equipment to keep food hot and containers for transport; with cook-chill or cook-freeze you need a blast chiller, cold rooms or cabinets with a temperature recorder and, where the meal is eaten, equipment to regenerate it.
  • In your country: the rules are different: check them with a local technician.

The kitchen zones

In Lombardy (Italy), kitchens that serve more than 100 meals a day and canteens have separate spaces to prepare (and, where needed, wash) meat, vegetables, cheese and cured meats, and first courses. The product follows the "forward flow", from delivery to consumption (Lombardy model hygiene regulation, art. 4.5.6, in Italian).

  • Goods in and storage: dry, chilled and frozen.
  • Separate preparation: meat, vegetables, cheese and cured meats, first courses.
  • Cooking: the machines depend on the menu and the number of meals; gas, electric or mixed.
  • Blast chilling and storage: if the food system calls for it.
  • Portioning and packing: for the meals served on site or sent out.
  • Special diets: in Italy, canteens in schools, hospitals and public facilities serve gluten-free meals to those who ask for them (Law 123/2005, art. 4, in Italian). For schools, the 2010 guidelines ask for each dish of the diet to be prepared in a dedicated area, even if only for the time of preparation, with utensils used only for that diet (p. 28).
  • Washing: dishes, pots and, if the meals travel, the returning containers.
  • Staff facilities: changing rooms and toilets according to local rules.

If the meals come from elsewhere: the satellite kitchen

Many canteens do not cook: they receive the meals from a central kitchen and serve them.

  • Keeping the temperature: in Lombardy (Italy), a satellite kitchen that receives cook & hold meals has equipment that keeps them at no less than +65 °C. With cook-chill or cook-freeze meals, it stores them at +2 °C or -18 °C, with a temperature recorder, and has appliances to bring them back to +65-70 °C, such as bain-maries, convection or steam ovens and microwaves (Lombardy model hygiene regulation, art. 4.5.12, in Italian).
  • Transport: in Italy, the school catering guidelines list insulated or heated containers and travel times kept to a minimum; in the dining hall, hot or cold counters, heated trolleys, active or passive insulated containers (National guidelines for school catering, 2010, p. 24-25, in Italian).
  • The "office": in Rome (Italy), the ASL (the local health authority) asks for an "office", the room where the meals arrive, are unpacked and portioned, with a hand basin with non-manual controls, washable worktops, a dishwashing area if washing is done on site, toilets and changing rooms. Below 100 meals, if the meals are only served there, the "office" can be a separate zone of the dining hall (ASL Roma 1, Guidelines 2021, p. 9, in Italian).
  • In your country: the rules are different: check them with a local technician.

The dining room

  • Workplace canteen: in Italy, if more than 30 employees stay at work during the meal break, a dining room is needed, with seats and tables, lit, ventilated and heated in winter. Those who bring a meal from home must be able to keep it, heat it and wash the container (Legislative Decree 81/2008, Annex IV, points 1.11.2 and 1.11.3, in Italian).
  • Space and noise: in Lombardy (Italy), the dining room of a workplace canteen has at least 1 m² per person, with materials and furniture that reduce noise (Lombardy model hygiene regulation, art. 3.11.10, in Italian).
  • School canteen: school buildings also have rules of their own: check them with the local authorities.
  • In your country: the rules are different: check them with a local technician.

Public tenders in Italy: the minimum environmental criteria

In public canteens run under contract, the minimum environmental criteria for collective catering apply (CAM, the Italian acronym) (Ministerial Decree of 10 March 2020, Official Gazette no. 90 of 4/4/2020, in Italian). Check the version in force. In your country public tenders have their own rules. Some points affect the design of the kitchen.

  • Reusable tableware: in school canteens, meals are eaten with glasses in glass or hard uncoloured plastic, plates in white ceramic or porcelain and stainless steel cutlery. If there is no dishwasher in the building and the space and services allow it, the operator installs one within three months. Similar rules apply to canteens in offices, universities and barracks.
  • Containers: those for storage at room temperature or in the fridge are reusable; insulated containers for transport are fully recyclable.
  • Dishwashers supplied with the contract: with heat recovery or connected directly to the mains hot water, double-walled and with built-in pre-wash.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing the machines before the food system: blast chiller, cold rooms, heated trolleys and regeneration depend on how the meal travels.
  • Forgetting the returning containers: they have to be washed and stored, and they take up space.
  • No zone for special diets in the canteens of schools, hospitals and public facilities.
  • A satellite kitchen without equipment to keep hot and cold the meals that arrive.
  • A noisy dining room: materials and furniture that do not absorb sound.

How to get a free preview

Brigaplan uses AI to generate a preview of the kitchen, and the engine checks it against the rules. The preview is for the kitchen that prepares the meals: a satellite kitchen that only receives them does not need it. Have this information ready:

  • Floor plan or description: upload the floor plan of your venue, or describe it in words with its dimensions.
  • What kind of canteen: who you cook for, how many meals you make a day and in how many sittings.
  • Energy and connections: gas, electric or mixed; the electrical power available in kW, which you find on your electricity bill or meter. If you don't know it yet, that's fine.
  • The city: hygiene rules change from place to place.
  • What you get: a preview without dimensions, brands or codes, with the engine's warnings.

The design is done online. Local rules must be checked with a local technician.
