Pastry kitchen: layout, equipment and space

A pastry kitchen works well if the product always moves forward: from the raw materials to the display case, without going back and without crossing the dirty side. Here you will find the zones, the legal minimums in some Italian regions as examples, the typical equipment and the mistakes to avoid.

The zones, in sequence

In Lombardy (Italy), the model hygiene regulation requires production lines to follow the order in which the product is worked, without mixing operations out of sequence (Lombardy model hygiene regulation, art. 4.2.7, in Italian).

  • Goods in and storage: flour, sugar and dry goods on one side; milk, cream, eggs and butter in the cold.
  • Weighing and mixing: scales, dough mixer and planetary mixer close to the flour store.
  • Working and shaping: workbenches, dough sheeter, cold worktops for pastry and chocolate.
  • Proofing: proofing cabinets or rooms, if you make leavened products.
  • Cooking: ovens, and burners for creams and syrups.
  • Cooling: tray trolleys and a blast chiller, before filling or storing.
  • Filling and decorating: the most delicate zone, with creams and whipped cream. It must be kept clean and away from the flour.
  • Finished products: fridges and storage, then the shop or the dining room.
  • Washing: trays, moulds and utensils in a separate zone, without crossing the filling area.

How much space you need

The minimums change from region to region. These are examples from Italy. In your country the rules are different: check them with a local technician and with the local authorities. Here are some Italian examples.

  • Lombardy: the production room has at least 2 m² net per worker and at least 8 m² in total. You also need a separate store for raw materials and finished products, a WC with an anteroom and a changing room (Lombardy model hygiene regulation, art. 4.2.4, in Italian).
  • Como, practice of the former ASL (the local health authority): 8 m² if the room is at least 3 m high, 9 m² if the height is between 2.70 and 3 m (Guidelines of the former ASL Como, in Italian).
  • Rome, practice of ASL Roma 1: fresh pastry at least 20 m², dry pastry at least 10 m². With semi-finished or ready-made products the ASL assesses case by case (ASL Roma 1, Guidelines 2021, p. 7, in Italian).
  • Rimini, municipal regulation: a production room attached to a shop has at least 20 m² and an average height of 3 m (Municipality of Rimini, Table 1, in Italian).
  • Your city: check with the local health authorities before signing the lease. The difference between 8 and 20 m² changes the venue.

The typical equipment

  • Ovens: convection (fan) ovens or deck (static) ovens. You can have both.
  • Mixing: a dough mixer for leavened doughs and a planetary mixer for creams, whipped mixtures and soft doughs.
  • Dough sheeter: for puff pastry, rolled shortcrust and croissants.
  • Refrigeration: refrigerated cabinets and counters, blast chiller, freezer. In pastry the standard is the 60×40 cm tray: ovens, cabinets and trolleys are chosen so that they take it.
  • Proofing: controlled proofing cabinets or rooms, to schedule leavened products.
  • Worktops: in stainless steel; for chocolate and sugar sometimes a stone worktop as well.
  • The real dimensions are those on the data sheet: before fixing the floor plan, take the dimensions from the technical data sheet of the chosen model.

Refrigeration and temperatures

  • Creams and whipped cream in the cold: in Italy the ASLs still refer to the values of DPR 327/1980, art. 31: at most +4 °C for products filled with whipped cream or with a cream made from eggs and milk. The decree was largely repealed in 2021: check with the ASL which values it applies (DPR 327/1980, art. 31, in Italian).
  • Cool quickly: after cooking, creams and bases are cooled straight away, for example in a blast chiller, before going into the fridge.
  • Readable thermometers: fridges and display cases with a thermometer you can read from outside.
  • In your country: the rules are different: check them with a local technician.

Flour, heat and air

  • Showers: in Lombardy (Italy), the regional regulation requires them where dust or heat make them advisable, in a number suited to the staff (Lombardy model hygiene regulation, art. 4.2.4, in Italian).
  • Emissions: in Italy, bakeries and pastry shops that use up to 300 kg of flour a day are activities of "minor significance" and do not need an emissions permit; above that, they do (Legislative Decree 152/2006, Part V, Annex IV, as reported in a circular from the Lombardy Region, in Italian). Check the version in force.
  • Hood: ovens and burners produce heat and steam. What to ask the technician is in the guide Commercial kitchen hood: size, airflow and ducting.
  • In your country: the rules are different: check them with a local technician.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Filling next to mixing: flour flies and ends up in the creams.
  • Washing that crosses the production room: dirty trays and finished products must not cross paths.
  • Undersized refrigeration: creams, bases and finished products need more fridge space than it seems.
  • A venue chosen without knowing the regional minimum.

How to get a free preview

Brigaplan uses AI to generate a preview of your pastry kitchen or kitchen, and the engine checks it against the rules. Have this information ready:

  • Floor plan or description: upload the floor plan of your venue, or describe it in words with its dimensions.
  • What you produce: fresh pastry, dry pastry, leavened products, chocolate, and whether there is a bar or a shop.
  • How many people work together in the pastry kitchen.
  • Energy and connections: gas, electric or mixed; the electrical power available in kW, which you find on your electricity bill or meter. If you don't know it yet, that's fine.
  • The city: the minimums change from place to place.
  • What you get: a preview without dimensions, brands or codes, with the engine's warnings.
