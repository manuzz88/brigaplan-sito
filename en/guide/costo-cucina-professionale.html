How much does commercial kitchen equipment cost

The cost of a commercial kitchen depends on four things: the type of venue, the number of covers, the menu and how much of the equipment you already have you can reuse. Here you find the orders of magnitude, taken from complete example designs made with Brigaplan. These are orders of magnitude for the Italian market (2026), in euros: in other countries price lists and prices may be different.

Orders of magnitude by type of venue

These are orders of magnitude for the equipment only, excluding VAT, installation and building services. The free preview gives you the figure for your venue.

How to read the table:

  • "From around" is the budget choice: equivalent equipment from the less expensive manufacturers.
  • "Upwards": with the same equipment from the more expensive brands, the cost can even double.
  • Equivalents are appliances with the same function, similar dimensions, the same capacity and the same energy supply.
  • The figures change with catalogues and price lists: that is why you find orders of magnitude here, not prices.
  • Sushi: rice cookers and steamers are not included.

What makes the price go up or down

  • Cooking line: more burners, fryers and griddles (fry-tops) mean a longer line and a longer hood. The series matters too: deeper, heavier-duty lines cost more.
  • Combi oven and blast chiller: they are among the most expensive items. They are almost always needed, but their size (number of GN trays) changes the price a lot.
  • Refrigeration: for small volumes, refrigerated cabinets and refrigerated counters are enough. A walk-in cold room is needed when volumes are high.
  • Dishwashing: a hood-type dishwasher is enough for many restaurants. Above certain volumes you need a rack conveyor dishwasher, which costs much more.
  • Gas or electric: the choice changes the price of the appliances and, above all, of the building services.
  • Equipment you already have: if it is in good condition, keeping it lowers the bill. But the design must take its dimensions and its connections into account.

What the figures do not include

  • installation and transport;
  • electrical, plumbing, gas and drainage systems;
  • building work, floors and wall coverings;
  • flue and extraction system beyond the hood;
  • dining room furniture, tableware and smallwares (pots, trays, utensils);
  • design, construction drawings, and local paperwork and permits.

Indicative price and real price

  • The figures are indicative: the price you pay depends on the dealer, the brands you choose and when you buy.
  • A tip: ask for quotes on the same design. That way you really compare prices, not different kitchens.

An estimate for your venue

With the free Brigaplan preview you upload the floor plan or describe the venue and you get an equipment estimate in three price ranges (budget, mid-range, premium), at full and indicative list prices, in total and by zone: transport, installation, connections, building services and VAT excluded. The engine calculates it on the equipment in your design. If you know them, also tell us whether you cook with gas or electricity and the electrical power available, which you find on your electricity bill: they change the equipment, and so the estimate. You see it straight away as an automatic draft, checked by the engine.
